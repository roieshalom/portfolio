Helping suppliers restock Wayfair through one unified order tool

As Product Design Lead on Wayfair's Partner Home, I unified four separate inbound-order tools into a consistent experience for all Wayfair's suppliers globally.

Overview

The brief

Context

Suppliers use Partner Home to ship stock into Wayfair's CastleGate warehouses, closer to customers for faster delivery. Over time, four teams had built four separate tools for that one job.

Problem

Suppliers dealt with inconsistent legacy experiences, missing regional rules and missed replenishment windows. Wayfair had to maintain four codebases for what was essentially one workflow.

Goals

  • Replace four separate tools with a single order-entry experience for every supplier type and region.
  • Retire buggy legacy systems and reduce support tickets
  • Bring the interface in line with the design system Wayfair was rolling out at the time.

Success criteria

Less than 10% suppliers reverting to the old system during transition. with a volume dip of no more than 5% in shipments. 

Challenges

Creating a new system that still answers all of the essentials needs for a diverse group of users. 

Solution

One order-entry tool with two paths: dense tables for power users and a guided wizard for everyone else, both built from a shared validation spec.

Discovery

Auditing the four tools

I started by laying the four tools side by side. Same job, four interfaces, each with its own fields, steps and gaps.

Discovery

Mapping the replenishment journey

Earlier research had mapped how each supplier segment restocks. The journeys were nearly identical, which made a single tool realistic rather than a compromise.

The replenishment journey was nearly identical across supplier segments.

Development

Turning the spec into building blocks

Before pushing new screens, I documented every field, error state and edge case as reusable validation blocks. Engineers built both flows from that one source, and it earned me the team's trust after a rough handoff.

Validation blocks: every field, error state and edge case in one spec.

Development

Designing two paths in one tool

  • Tables for power users: dense, spreadsheet-like views with inline editing, bulk actions, search and filters.
  • A wizard for everyone else: step-by-step choices that say what they do.

Both paths run on one codebase.

In the wizard, every option explains itself. Not just "Truckload" but "Move products from your warehouses to Wayfair's facilities." Guiding users means explaining their non-selections too.

The wizard's first step. Each shipment type says what it actually does.

Validation

Testing with suppliers

I ran 3 research waves, contacting 123 suppliers and interviewing 11 across the EU and North America.

  • It worked: 100% completed the task unassisted, in about 4 minutes on average.
  • One blind spot: every participant first tried the table search, expecting a catalog search. Finding "Manage Products" took up to 70% of some sessions.

A local fix for "Manage Products" would have broken design-system consistency, and the system rollout was about to solve it. I escalated, explored alternatives, and we kept the design.

Every participant finished the task. Every participant hit the same blind spot first.

Delivery

Rolling out in five phases

We released in 5 phases across the US, Canada and Europe, one supplier segment at a time. Each phase was monitored so order volume held steady and reversion stayed low.

Outcomes

Who I worked with

  • Product management: worked through three PM changes before the role stabilized.
  • Engineering: ~6 engineers in Boston built both flows from my validation blocks, across a 6-hour time gap.
  • Design system team: escalated the "Manage Products" finding and explored alternative placements together.
  • Suppliers: 11 interviews over 3 research waves shaped and validated the flows.

Key takeaways

What I'd keep, what I'd change

What worked

  • A shared validation system as the single spec.
  • Guardrail metrics on every release.

What I'd do differently

  • Secure time for post-launch iteration.
  • Align earlier with parallel design teams.
